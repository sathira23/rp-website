Research Domain

This research focuses on a smart highway safety platform that combines RFID vehicle identification, live speed calculation, machine learning risk prediction, driver-state monitoring, accident response, and real-time dashboard alerts.

RFID Telemetrics Speed Monitoring ML Prediction Accident Response

Research Foundation

The study addresses the academic problem, existing gap, and project objectives behind the smart highway monitoring system.

Literature Survey

Traditional traffic monitoring systems mostly detect violations after they happen using cameras, manual checking, or isolated speed sensors. Modern intelligent transport research shows that IoT, RFID, dashboards, and machine learning can support faster identification, prediction, and emergency response.

Research Gap

Many existing systems identify a vehicle, but they do not reliably connect the vehicle with the current driver, driver condition, speed behavior, and historical risk.

Research Problem

Highway accidents and speed violations increase when authorities receive late information about overspeeding, risky drivers, abnormal driver conditions, or accident events.

Core Research Components

Each component contributes a specific intelligence layer to improve road safety and enforcement response.

Research Objectives

Identify vehicles and drivers using RFID, calculate speed between checkpoints, classify driver risk, detect accidents quickly, and provide real-time alerts through a professional dashboard.

RFID Speed Monitoring

Entry checkpoint Z scans the vehicle and selected driver. Checkpoints A and B match the same driver and calculate speed using distance and time. Exit checkpoint C records final trip status.

Prediction Component

Machine learning analyzes speed, speed limit, time, location, vehicle type, traffic density, and past violations to classify low, medium, or high driver risk.

Driver And Accident Safety

RFID telemetrics, AI vision, and driver-state monitoring detect sudden speed drops, collisions, drowsiness, distraction, and risky behavior before or during incidents.

System Methodology

The complete workflow connects highway RFID checkpoints, ESP32 devices, a Node.js backend, MongoDB Atlas storage, machine learning analysis, and a live dashboard for authorities.

1

Entry Zone

The Z-location ESP32 scans the vehicle RFID, scans human RFID tags, selects the correct driver, and displays the vehicle entry event.

2

Speed Zone

The A and B RFID scanners identify the same vehicle and driver, then calculate speed from checkpoint distance and timestamp difference.

3

Exit Zone

At checkpoint C, the system displays exit driver, exit time, and status. The trip record is finalized for storage and review.

4

Dashboard Alerts

The dashboard shows driver selection, vehicle entry, live speed, exit details, risk levels, accident alerts, and statistics.

5

Hardware

The prototype uses ESP32 boards, MFRC522 RFID readers, AMS1117 3.3V power regulation, RFID vehicle tags, and driver tags.

6

Technologies Used

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Node.js, REST API, MongoDB Atlas, ESP32, MFRC522 RFID, machine learning, dashboard visualization, and AI vision concepts.
